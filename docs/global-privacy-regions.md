## Recommendation and method

Recommend **Zurich (`eu-central-2`)** as the next privacy-focused Ghostline candidate. The ordered shortlist across all AWS partitions is:

| Rank | Region | Main reason | Material qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Zurich, `eu-central-2` | Strong Swiss privacy law, a separate legal jurisdiction from the EU, and demonstrated ability for a Swiss VPN operator to run without connection logs. | Swiss cable interception and lawful access exist; surveillance-ordinance reform remains a live risk. AWS does not inherit Proton's corporate/legal position. |
| 2 | European Sovereign Cloud (Germany), `eusc-de-east-1` | Adds EU-only operations, EU-local customer-created metadata and stronger contractual access boundaries to German/EU protections. | Separate partition/account and unqualified Ghostline service/platform support. Sovereignty controls are not immunity from compelled disclosure or German intelligence powers. Placement here is the least certain ranking judgment. |
| 3 | Stockholm, `eu-north-1` | Strong EU privacy baseline and judicial/independent oversight, with the specific bulk-surveillance safeguard deficiencies remedied. | FRA bulk interception and provider retention duties remain. This is a good practical balance, not a surveillance-free jurisdiction. |
| 4 | Frankfurt, `eu-central-1` | Strong constitutional protection, including for foreigners abroad, and effective judicial challenges to indiscriminate retention. | BND strategic surveillance remains; 2026 IP-retention and intelligence-expansion bills are adverse policy signals. German destination age checks conflict with Anthony's earlier access preference. |
| 5 | Ireland, `eu-west-1` | EU privacy rights, courts and data-protection oversight make it a reasonable conventional-region alternative. | Legislation permits broad national-security retention, and government has actually obtained such orders. Do not market it as a no-retention haven. |

These are **ordinal judgments**, not measured privacy scores or estimates of interception probability. Prioritize constraints on government/provider access, collection and retention; legal remedies and treatment of a foreign user; provider operational exposure; then internet openness. Latency and price do not determine this privacy ranking. Positions 2–5 are close and threat-model dependent. German sovereign operations are ranked separately because this is a region comparison, not a five-country list. If constrained to the current ordinary AWS partition/account, the order becomes **Zurich, Stockholm, Frankfurt, Ireland, Spain**. Spain is the next conventional candidate, with explicit retention and blocking caveats below.

“Best available AWS region for this personal VPN” is the claim. No region establishes anonymity against a capable state or makes Ghostline the world's most private VPN. Privacy law protecting personal-data processing, surveillance powers over communications, and destination-site age assurance are different questions. The last can still impose identity disclosure even from the highest-ranked server location.

## Finalist evidence and uncertainty

### Zurich

The revised Swiss [Federal Act on Data Protection](https://www.bj.admin.ch/en/new-data-protection-legislation) took effect September 1, 2023. It provides a strong personal-data baseline, rather than a guarantee against surveillance. The [Swiss privacy regulator](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/en/telecommunications-confidentiality-and-surveillance-of-telecommunications) explicitly explains criminal and intelligence interception powers. Cross-border cable intelligence exists; Switzerland's absence from the five-country Five Eyes group does not imply absence of intelligence cooperation.

[Proton's July 2026 transparency report](https://protonvpn.com/blog/transparency-report) states Swiss law does not oblige **Proton VPN** to keep connection logs, and that it could not provide identifying records in 458 approved requests since 2017. This is provider-reported operational evidence, not an AWS legal exemption, an independent audit of Ghostline, or a general promise that a Swiss server cannot be monitored.

The Swiss government [announced on February 11, 2026](https://www.big.admin.ch/de/newnsb/EXv-JkPBAZuL) that contested VÜPF/VD-ÜPF revisions were being reworked with an impact assessment and another consultation. Its [September 28 parliamentary answer, 26.7879](https://ws-old.parlament.ch/affairs/20267879) says work was not yet finished; the indexed German answer plans another committee information letter for November. The default rendered page returned French with a note that the substantive response exists only in German; the German answer was available in search indexing. No final enactment of the contested expansion was established in this review. Treat the proposal as a policy risk, not enacted universal VPN logging, and recheck the final official text before launch. The current top choice is consequently provisional, with moderate rather than absolute confidence.

### Germany: sovereign region and Frankfurt

The [May 14, 2026 sovereign addendum](https://www.aws.eu/esca/) contractually restricts operation/support to sovereign-cloud staff and says personnel outside the EU have no technical means to access customer content/customer-created metadata. It requires efforts to redirect requests, notify where permitted, and challenge conflicting/overbroad requests. **Sections 2.1(b) and 2.2 still permit disclosure under valid binding orders after those steps.** It does not promise that US law can never reach data. [AWS's November 1 account of its October 24 exercise](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/inside-a-successful-aws-european-sovereign-cloud-exercise-to-demonstrate-independent-operation/) reports operation without critical non-EU dependencies; EU-only residency of staff is current, while exclusive EU-citizen operation is described as a transition. These are AWS claims/contractual commitments, not a Ghostline native qualification.

Germany's [2020 constitutional ruling](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2020/bvg20-037.html) extends telecommunications fundamental-rights constraints to German surveillance of foreigners abroad. The court also permits a constitutionally designed strategic-surveillance framework. The [2023 administrative judgment](https://www.bverwg.de/pm/2023/66) rejected the then-applicable indiscriminate-retention scheme. Neither ruling abolishes lawful surveillance.

Two current adverse signals matter: the [October 2026 Bundestag hearing](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw41-pa-recht-ip-adressspeicherung-1221516) on proposed three-month IP-assignment retention, and the [government's intelligence-reform explanation](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/bundesregierung/bundeskanzleramt/bnd-nachrichtendienste-reform-2449400), which identifies the August cabinet measure as still in parliamentary proceedings. Treat them as proposals on this evidence, not confirmed enacted obligations. The sovereign partition changes AWS operations, not German communications law or the age-assurance policies discussed in [region selection](region-selection.md).

### Stockholm

FRA operates a bulk-interception regime. Avoid repeating the 2021 ECHR violation as an uncorrected finding: the [Council of Europe closed supervision in June 2025](https://www.coe.int/uk/web/execution/-/sweden-committee-of-ministers-ends-its-supervision-of-a-case-concerning-shortcomings-of-the-swedish-system-of-bulk-interception-of-communications) after 2024 amendments remedied the identified destruction, foreign-sharing privacy and independent-review shortcomings. That establishes specific safeguards improved; it does not abolish bulk interception. [PTS's current retention explanation](https://www.pts.se/sakerhet-och-integritet/brottsbekampning/datalagring/) confirms obligations for covered communications providers. The [PTS service-scope explanation](https://pts.se/tillstand-och-anmalan/internet-och-telefoni/anmalningsplikt/) distinguishes public services from private/ancillary arrangements. No inference is made that a personal Ghostline server must collect commercial subscriber logs.

### Ireland

The [2022 amendment commenced in June 2023](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-justice-home-affairs-and-migration/press-releases/minister-mcentee-commences-act-to-facilitate-retention-of-data-to-protect-national-security-and-tackle-serious-crime-2/) allows general retention on national-security grounds with designated-judge approval. Government [confirmed obtaining a 12-month order](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-justice-home-affairs-and-migration/press-releases/minister-mcentee-obtains-high-court-order-requiring-retention-of-data-for-state-security/). This is a real mechanism and observed use, not proof that that particular order still operates in November 2026. EU rules, courts and oversight make Ireland competitive, but claims that it has no broad retention would be misleading. Its preference over Japan, Taiwan, Canada or Spain is a lower-confidence judgment about the combined legal framework, not a measured surveillance advantage.

## What region selection does not fix

The ordinary AWS account remains identifiable to AWS through account/billing and operational information. The [CLOUD Act](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/cloud-act/) can reach data in a covered provider's possession/custody/control regardless of server location, subject to legal process and challenges. AWS reports no disclosures of enterprise/government customer content outside the US to the US government since reporting began; the dated statistic on the page is June 2025, and it is neither an immunity guarantee nor a statement that no account metadata is disclosed. It should not be generalized to all personal-account data or all surveillance authorities.

Nitro's workload protections matter; the provider boundary does not imply AWS operators can freely read VM memory or TLS content. Network timing/address metadata and explicitly collected telemetry remain distinct. [GuardDuty's own event specification](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/guardduty/latest/ug/runtime-monitoring-collected-events.html) includes DNS packet payloads and local/remote connection endpoints sent to its backend. Ghostline's upstream DoT remains encrypted; the potential DNS collection point is the private host bridge after VPN decryption and before CoreDNS's TLS forwarder. Actual export of particular forwarded queries by the deployed agent has not been established. [DNS visibility and evidence limits](guardduty.md#dns-over-tls-and-runtime-visibility) distinguish that risk from foundational AWS Resolver logs, which this external-resolver path bypasses. The current [product privacy requirement](product.md#acceptance-and-privacy) already records the telemetry boundary and unfinished resolver/container-log audit. Do not disable security controls silently or describe relocating as completing the zero-log project. A dedicated personal EIP also lacks the crowd-sharing property of a commercial shared VPN exit; that is an anonymity limitation independent of country.

Transit can cross other jurisdictions; server location does not confine the complete network path. HTTPS still protects application content beyond the VPN, while the tunnel terminates at the exit and does not hide account logins, browser identifiers or all destination metadata. No fresh latency, routing, destination access, leak check, instance/AMI/service availability or live cloud state was measured in this research; historical September access results are explicitly dated in the head-to-head above.

For the requested three-role portfolio, Zurich is now the additional deployed testing candidate, Oregon remains the US exit and Stockholm remains the existing MENA-oriented option. See [Zurich acceptance and limits](launch-zurich.md). Before any selected launch, refresh Swiss surveillance changes and destination age-assurance implications, perform ordinary regional preflight/diff, and retain the same qualified security controls. Treat European Sovereign Cloud as a separate feasibility/qualification project rather than adding it to the ordinary regional catalog on research alone.
